GitHub repository trust

A public URL, a trust score

Paste any public GitHub repository. The app returns a score from 0 to 100, a tier, and the signals behind them.

The question

Does this look like a known-good repository?

The score is how close public metadata sits to repositories such as widely used, maintained projects. A low score means limited evidence of that resemblance.

It is not a finding that the repository is harmful, malicious, or unfit to use.

The app

One page, three things to read

  • Score. 0 to 100, or withheld when the comparison would be misleading.
  • Tier. Established, Mixed, or Limited evidence.
  • Signals. 18 rows. Each one says whether it raised the score, lowered it, or barely moved it.

Repository files are never downloaded and never run.

One lookup

From a URL to a score

  1. 1Read a github.com URL, or an owner/name pair.
  2. 2Fetch the repository, the owner profile, and daily star history.
  3. 3Turn that metadata into the same 18 features used in training.
  4. 4Run the saved logistic regression. No retraining at lookup time.
  5. 5Apply the tier rules, then return the breakdown.

What GitHub is asked for

Three public reads

  • Repository. Stars, forks, watchers, open issues, size, dates, license, and whether the owner is an organization.
  • Owner. Follower count, used for stars per owner follower. If this call fails, that one feature uses a typical training value.
  • Star history. Stars per day. The lookup waits 4 seconds. If history is slow or missing, the score still returns.

The repository payload is cached for 24 hours. The client pauses before it uses up the hourly rate limit.

Features

Eighteen signals, five groups

  • Audience. Stars, forks, watchers, open issues, size.
  • Time. Age in days, days since the last push, stars per day.
  • Ratios. Forks, watchers, and open issues per star, plus stars per owner follower.
  • Identity. A detected license, and an organization owner.
  • Star timing. The busiest day, that day's share of all stars, the share of days with at least one star, and burstiness.

Training

A small, human-reviewed set

  • 106 repositories. 82 labeled known-good, 24 labeled uncertain.
  • Uncertain means thin history, weak engagement for the star count, or heavy marketing. It does not mean proven harm.
  • Split. 80 percent train, 20 percent holdout, stratified, seed 42. That is 84 train rows and 22 holdout rows.
  • Fit. Fill gaps with the training median, standardize each column, then fit a logistic regression.
  • Holdout ROC AUC. 0.965. The holdout has 17 known-good repos and 5 uncertain ones, so that number is from a small test.

The model

One weighted sum, then a curve

For each signal

z = (value - mean) / scale
contribution = coefficient x z

A missing value is replaced with the training median. The row is still marked as not loaded.

Then the score

logit = 2.90 + sum of contributions
probability = 1 / (1 + exp(-logit))
score = round(probability x 100)

The score is clamped to 0 through 100. The probability is P(known-good).

How a row gets its arrow

Each signal's addition to the logit

  • Raises the score when that signal's contribution is above +0.05.
  • Lowers the score when the contribution is below -0.05.
  • Little effect when it sits between those two.
  • Typical value when the live number was not loaded. The median still entered the sum.

A repo at the training average has logit 2.90, about a score of 95. That average repo has about 52,500 stars and is 2,950 days old, and about 88% of the training repos are organization-owned. Live repos are scored by how far they sit from that center. A star count of at least 1,000 is not allowed to lower the score, because that center is much higher than a popular repository in the wider world.

Standardized coefficients

What moves the score most

  • Org owner +1.20
  • Age in days +0.91
  • Busiest-day share -0.86
  • Days since push -0.67
  • Days with a star +0.64
  • Watchers per star +0.54
  • Stars on busiest day -0.48
  • Stars per follower -0.47

Bar length is the absolute coefficient. A plus sign means a higher value raises P(known-good), after the other signals. These are associations in a small training set.

Star timing

A calendar of daily star counts

Days run from the creation date through yesterday. A day with no stars is a zero, not a gap in the series.

  • Busiest day. The largest single-day count.
  • That day's share. Busiest day divided by all stars in the window.
  • Active-day share. Days with at least one star, divided by all days.
  • Burstiness. (standard deviation - mean) / (standard deviation + mean) of the daily counts. Steady calendars sit near -1. One or two spikes sit near +1.

A launch day and a coordinated campaign can look alike here. The other 14 signals stay in the sum.

The last step

The number is sometimes withheld

Established

70 to 100. Metadata resembles the known-good training repos.

Mixed

40 to 69. Some signals match that set and some do not.

Limited evidence

Under 40, or the score is blank. Weak resemblance, not a harm verdict.

Under 90 days old, or under 50 stars, the score stays blank and the tier is Limited evidence. The training set is mostly well-known projects, so a low number there would mostly measure age and audience.

An archived repo or a fork adds a warning. Neither one is its own feature in the model.
